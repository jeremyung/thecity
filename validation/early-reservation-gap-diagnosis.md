The binding mechanism in these examples is the timing of parcel admission. Roads receive frontage lots as they open; the world later has fully occupied housing and active commerce, and there is no empty parcel stock. Once surrounding lots seal the interior, exact collision checks correctly reject new lots and the current bounded access recipe finds no admissible branch. The finite boundary, protected terrain, river banks, and ordinary road clearance rule out some other sampled space, but these three faces fail first on existing parcel reservations. Current demand is high and resource caps have substantial headroom, so neither weak demand nor a reached cap explains the halt.

## What an earlier access reservation would have to change

The two prior access experiments rule out a generic scheduling tweak. The early-access scheduler offered the existing access planner after normal usable-frontage/building work had priority. It admitted three access plans in each of seeds 0 and 87,231; projected kit coverage changed by +2.16% and +0.013%, respectively. The road-opening treatment tried access when a connected road completed, before adding that road's frontage, and produced 30 and 36 admissions. Yet projected kit coverage fell by 1.41% and 6.32%. The face-conditioned opening experiment additionally required a newly bounded face with sampled unserved interior: on seed 0 it admitted two plans in 60 attempts, while projected kit coverage increased only 65.25 m² (0.055%). See [early-access-scheduling-experiment.md](early-access-scheduling-experiment.md), [road-opening-rendered-coverage.md](road-opening-rendered-coverage.md), and [road-opening-access-experiment.md](road-opening-access-experiment.md); the face-filtered run is in [face-conditioned-access-seed-0-round-robin-matched.json](face-conditioned-access-seed-0-round-robin-matched.json) and its paired coverage files.

Those opening hooks happen in `advance_projects`: after the road is marked open and connectivity is recomputed, but immediately before `add_frontage_parcels` adds that road's lots. They still run after earlier sides of the face have opened and admitted frontage. The face-conditioned version changes which already-open road candidates it samples, not when the surrounding perimeter first consumes parcels. Simply moving either access call earlier in `evaluate_growth` would also repeat the rejected early-access scheduler.

There is already a distinct earlier boundary for coordinated neighborhoods. `evaluate_growth` calls `try_reserve_early_coordinated_block` on its 80-tick cadence before the ordinary infill and active-building return paths. That function reserves a `NeighborhoodPlan` but does not start road work. `try_create_neighborhood_plan` then chooses a complete perimeter geometry before its first perimeter road can open or receive frontage. The existing optional `NeighborhoodPlan.spine` can reserve a canonical internal street and lots at that point. This is the only access-reservation timing change supported by the current architecture that is materially earlier than the rejected road-opening hook.

A meaningful face-led variant therefore cannot be “run the same 48 interior-access proposals at an earlier tick.” It must change the candidate being selected: at neighborhood-plan selection, compare a conventional perimeter candidate with a compound perimeter-plus-interior-street candidate, using the prospective block geometry before any of its new perimeter frontages are admitted. The candidate must reserve its corridor and full selected-lot set in that same plan, protect those reservations from all frontage and road planners, then use the existing visible construction/split lifecycle and admit parcels only after the interior road opens. Existing parcels on reused perimeter edges remain obstacles; no face projection may erase or overlap them. If the planner instead waits for a newly closed face and then invokes the existing access proposal function, it repeats the prior opening-time treatment.

The bounded search already available at this boundary is at most 24 anchors, with separate shared-edge and full-block candidate budgets of 32 each and a spine budget of 24. Preserve the persisted neighborhood-anchor cursor and allocate IDs only for the winning candidate. The current minimum admission gates are demand at least 1.1, at least 10 active buildings, and population at least 24; the world must also have no pending access or neighborhood plan and enough room for every reserved road, project, and parcel. Keep those gates live at admission and again before construction/completion. The early reservation may coexist with unrelated building work, but it must not manufacture occupancy or bypass the existing project, demand, parcel, road, protected-land, river, bounds, and collision checks.

There is an important ranking limitation. The current nominal-area-per-road score gives an ordinary 82×50 candidate 6.8900 m²/m versus 6.3618 m²/m for its full-perimeter 82×82 spine variant; with an 82 m shared edge, the scores are 9.9943 versus 7.9522 m²/m. So a fully feasible ordinary candidate wins before the spine is admitted. A two-seed site-count comparator did select spines but had mixed rendered outcomes: seed 0 fell from 113,633.25 to 109,872.5 m² projected kit coverage, while seed 87,231 rose from 103,176.5 to 106,184 m². That experiment does not support promoting the spine or quietly increasing its weight. The missing policy decision is what measurable neighborhood benefit should justify the extra street when it reduces parcel-envelope area; this needs explicit review before another experiment. A face-aware selector is meaningfully different only if it evaluates the whole proposed neighborhood before frontage and demonstrates a benefit over the ordinary candidate on matched seeds. The present mature-world candidate scan provides no evidence that a later opening-time access retry can fill the three measured voids.

For the next experiment, preserve identical controls and compare seeds 0 and 87,231 at development rate 16 through tick 242,000, then extend to 7, 41, and 2026 if the first pair shows a coherent benefit. Record selected and completed compound plans, connected access, occupied housing, active commerce, ordinary road length, actual rendered-kit coverage, and canonical save/resume. Also include a rejection case where no branch and at least two valid lots fit; the ordinary perimeter plan must remain eligible, so an impossible spine cannot stall development. If no stated and tested benefit can outrank the area score without arbitrary weighting, do not recommend repeating the experiment.

For the already-built mature faces, a pre-frontage reservation cannot reclaim corridors now covered by occupied parcel envelopes. Doing that would require a separate, explicit redevelopment lifecycle with tenant relocation, history, and resumable construction; it must not silently remove or overlap current buildings.

The face polygons and unbuilt-area masks come from the UI's 2 m raster analysis and are approximate. Candidate generation uses the engine's current bounded frontage and access proposal functions; it does not enumerate arbitrary lot rotations, every point along every road, or all possible access streets. This report therefore diagnoses the current planner's failure mode and a concrete next opportunity, rather than claiming that all unbuilt ground is developable or that the proposed face-led reservation guarantees higher visual density.
