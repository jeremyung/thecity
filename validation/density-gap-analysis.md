# Density gap diagnosis

Measured 2026-10-09 from `validation/mature-world.candidate.json` (seed 87231, development rate 16, tick 242,000). This is a WASM-produced snapshot, not a claim that its visual result has passed the release gate.

The main gap is district-scale coverage and block depth, rather than a uniform spacing problem between adjacent parcels. All 405 buildings are open and have a parcel; the 405 parcel envelopes total 70,574 m². Against the full 800 × 800 m simulation domain (640,000 m²), that is 11.0% gross parcel-envelope coverage. The building-center bounding box is 453.6 × 780 m (353,800 m²), where those envelopes cover 20.0%. This latter rectangle is only a rough local denominator: it includes streets, river and other open space, and the save does not export a net developable-land polygon. The first three 100 m columns from the west contain no building centers (24 of 64 domain tiles); road geometry reaches x = −184 m while buildings begin at x = −66 m. The connected network therefore spans farther west than its built frontage.

At the parcel scale, the nearest oriented parcel-envelope edge gap has a 2.0 m median and 12.9 m p90; 69 of 405 buildings have a nearest envelope gap over 10 m, one exceeds 25 m, and none exceed 50 m. The typical lot is 14 × 12 m (168 m²; p10 and p90 are both 168 m²), with a 15 m frontage-center offset from its road. The renderer’s primary building body uses about 84% of parcel width and depth, while its ground paving uses 96%; the envelope-gap statistic is therefore optimistic for visible wall-to-wall continuity. These are parcel/kit geometry measurements, not image-space measurements. They show that shrinking the existing 2 m envelope separation alone would have limited effect: many green patches sit behind narrow roadside strips, and the road/building footprint covers only a small part of the developed extent.

The growth shape is strongly frontage-led. The save has 125 open road segments total: 123 ordinary segments (122 local and 1 arterial), plus 1 bridge and 1 rail segment. The ordinary network averages 70.6 m per segment; all open centerlines total 9.0 km. Ordinary streets have 402 frontage records, or 3.27 per segment on average. Seventeen ordinary segments have zero or one frontage and 53 have at most two. A typical 70 m ordinary segment has three candidate positions per side at 16 m spacing, but initial parcel creation accepts only midpoint small lots on ordinary roads: at most two of those per segment, with a separately reserved tower envelope sometimes taking one midpoint slot. Further sites are scanned incrementally and require demand ≥1.2 plus at least four nearby open buildings and three occupied/active buildings within 78 m. The 2 m parcel-overlap buffer, river/bank clearance, protected rectangle, road-centerline clearance (at least 9 m, and 11 m for a 14 × 12 lot), and tower reservations remove otherwise aligned candidates. The save records accepted parcels, not rejected candidates, so historical rejection counts cannot be reconstructed from this snapshot.

There is one completed neighborhood block plan: an 82 × 50 m loop whose four road segments have `next_segment = 4`. Those roads carry 12 parcels total (6, 1, 5, and 0); the remaining 390 of 402 frontage records belong to other roads. In source, `neighborhood_plan` is a single `Option<NeighborhoodPlan>`. Both `try_create_neighborhood_plan()` and the scheduler require it to be `None`, while the completed plan is not cleared. This makes a second block plan impossible for the lifetime of the save. That is the clearest structural constraint behind the shortage of multi-block fabric. The plan also supplies parcel dimensions/spacing and is the sole source used by `is_neighborhood_plan_road()` to classify its roads. Simply clearing the option would silently reclassify historic block roads as ordinary and change their future infill rules.

The next growth change should rotate a bounded active block plan through qualifying connected urban edges while persisting historical block-road classification separately. Admit a new loop or interior access street only when local occupied demand, a viable interior parcel count, and network value justify it; then fill compact 16 m parcels around the loop before extending another distant road. Keep the existing demand, access, river, protected-land, tower, and construction constraints. A before/after evaluation should report parcel-envelope and floor-area coverage both gross and within the actual developed district, interior accessible sites, occupied sites, frontage continuity, and new road length/cost. This targets the measured broad sparse extent without assuming that every green area should be built over.

Reproduce the metrics with `node scripts/audit/analyze-density-gaps.mjs validation/mature-world.candidate.json`; run its geometry checks with `node scripts/audit/analyze-density-gaps.mjs --self-test`. The script computes nearest envelope separation by building each oriented width × depth rectangle from center and rotation, checking all edge pairs for intersection or minimum segment distance, then retaining each building’s nearest neighbor. The world-corner transform matches `group.rotation.y = b.rotation` in the renderer. Geometry checks cover a 90° asymmetric rectangle, containment, touching, and separated rectangles. Analysis emits JSON to stdout and does not mutate the save.

The target remains to be judged against the concept references, not inferred from these counts.
